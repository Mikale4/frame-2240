# Title Medallion — Art Brief

**For an external artist or image model producing the circular backdrop that sits
behind the title on the title screen of *F.R.A.M.E. 2240*.**

The subject is **STILLWAKE YARD** — a floating city that no longer exists, shown
as it was.

This document is self-contained and assumes no access to the codebase. Every
number in §4 is read out of the running game and is authoritative — the art has
to fit the engine, not the other way round.

**Attach the four existing stage layers alongside this file.** They are the
authority on style, and they matter more than any adjective in §3: the medallion
has to look like it was painted by the same hand, on the same day, for the same
world. If the reference images and this text ever disagree, the images win.
**Match the *far* layer's treatment most closely** — desaturated, low contrast,
dissolving into haze. That is the register this piece lives in.

---

## 0. The prompt to paste

**Self-contained — paste it on its own, with the stage layer images attached.**
Attach this file only if the model asks for more context.

```
I am attaching reference images: the parallax background layers for a 2D game
stage. I need a new piece of art for the same game, set in the same world, in
exactly the same style as those references.

Part 1 - The Subject

STILLWAKE YARD: a floating city, seen from a distance, at night, intact.

In this world the ground is gone - a permanent storm layer covers a dead
surface, and everything that survives is built on structures suspended in the
cold clear air two kilometres above it. The largest of those structures are
called yards: enormous lattices of girder and plate, each one a working city
carrying docks, foundries, cargo handling and a resident population. They hold
their altitude on reactor-driven lift spars.

Stillwake was one of these. Nine thousand people lived on it. It was held aloft
by a hundred and forty lift spars. It was a home, a port and an industrial
plant at the same time, and it is depicted here INTACT and INHABITED and LIT -
the way it was before it was lost. Nothing in the image is damaged, burning,
falling or tilted. This is a memory of a place, not a disaster.

Part 2 - Art Direction

Match the attached reference layers: same lighting logic, same colour harmony,
same structural vocabulary, same rust and wear treatment. This must read as the
same world drawn by the same hand. Match the FAR BACKGROUND layer most closely -
desaturated, low contrast, dissolving into haze.

Grounded industrial science fiction. Girder, plate, rivet, cable, truss, mast,
crane, sodium lamp. Not fantasy, not clean white utopian sci-fi, not cyberpunk
neon, not steampunk. Heavy honest load-bearing engineering. Painterly
semi-realistic rendering.

Render it as a SEMI-SILHOUETTE: the city is a large dark desaturated blue-grey
mass, read mainly as shape, but with real structure legible inside that mass -
tiers, trusses, hulls, cranes, the long downward rake of the lift spars beneath
it. Detailed, but low contrast. Think of a distant city at night in haze: you
can see how it is built, but everything is dim and nothing is crisp.

Its lights are small and warm - thousands of tiny sodium window and lamp points
scattered through the structure, densest in the lower and outer districts.
These are the only warm notes in the image and they must stay small. No glow
bloom, no light shafts, no lens flare.

Below and behind it: cold thin air, and far underneath, the top of an endless
storm layer. Above it: cold empty void.

Strict side view, orthographic, flat 2D elevation. No perspective convergence,
no vanishing point, no three-quarter view, no aerial view.

No text. No lettering. No numbers. No logos. No characters. No people. No
creatures. No aircraft. No mecha. No robots.

Part 3 - What To Make

A single square image containing one CIRCULAR composition: the city contained
within a circle, like a round window onto it.

- Square canvas, 1:1 aspect ratio, 2048x2048.
- Fully transparent background outside the circular composition.
- The imagery must FADE OUT softly toward the edge of the circle into full
  transparency. Do not draw a hard circular border, a ring, a frame, or a rim.
  There should be no crisp edge anywhere - the circle is defined by where the
  art fades away, not by an outline.
- The circle should fill most of the square, leaving a small transparent margin.

Composition rules, which matter more than the subject:

- THE CENTRE MUST BE QUIET. Large lettering is placed on top of the middle of
  this image. The central horizontal third should be the emptiest,
  lowest-contrast, least-detailed part of the picture. Compose so that this
  band is open air, haze, or the flattest and dimmest part of the structure.
  No bright window clusters, no crisp edges, no busy linework, no strong
  silhouette boundary running through the middle of the image.
- SIT THE CITY LOW. Put the main mass of the yard in the lower half, with its
  lift spars raking down out of the bottom of the circle. Let masts, cranes and
  upper trusses rise into the upper third. The middle stays the calmest.
- KEEP THE CENTRE COLD. The warm window lights belong low and around the
  outside. The middle of the image must stay cold blue-grey - warm tones there
  would destroy the readability of what sits on top of it.
- LOW CONTRAST OVERALL. This is a faded memory, not a crisp illustration.
  Deliver it at full opacity; it will be faded down in the engine.

Part 4 - Also Useful

Provide a second version with the same composition but the city mass pushed
slightly darker and denser, so the two can be compared against the lettering.

No text.
```

## 1. What this is for, in one paragraph

The title screen shows the game's name as two blocks of drawn lettering on a
diagonal — `F.R.A.M.E.` high and left, `2240` low and right — over a slowly
turning train of gears. Behind all of that sits a circular backdrop, currently a
piece of placeholder line art. This brief replaces it.

**It is a backdrop, not an illustration.** Nothing in it should ask to be looked
at. If a viewer notices the medallion before they notice the title, it is wrong.

## 2. Why Stillwake, and why intact

Worth understanding, because it changes how the picture should feel — but
**none of it may appear as text, caption or symbol in the art.**

`2240` in the title is not a year. It is the number of people who were taken off
Stillwake alive.

Stillwake Yard was held by the Emberline Pact, was inhabited, and was entered for
review as a military target. On the eleventh of the fourth, 31 CE, a single
experimental airframe was sent to test whether one machine operating outside a
yard's defensive envelope could drive its lift network into cascade. It could.
The cascade propagated in four minutes. Recovery became impossible nineteen
minutes later. Stillwake settled through the remainder of the day, entered the
storm layer, and continued below it. Evacuation lift removed 2,240 people before
access failed. The remaining population was lost with the yard.

Two things follow for the art:

* **It is drawn intact, lit and populated.** The place in the picture is gone,
  and showing it whole is the entire point — the image is what the number
  counts. A burning or falling yard would be melodrama, and would also spoil a
  reveal the game withholds until late.
* **The warmth is the subject.** CATHEDRAL YARD, the stage the game is fought
  on, is the cold empty one: lights on, nobody home. Stillwake is its opposite
  and that contrast is deliberate. Nine thousand people were awake in there.

**A first-time player must be able to read this image as "a cool sci-fi city"
and nothing more.** It only becomes the other thing once they have read the
Archive. Do not help them; no memorial framing, no dates, no names, no symbols.

## 3. Style authority

**The attached stage layers are the style guide.** Match them on:

| Property | What to match |
| --- | --- |
| Lighting | Cold key from above, warm sodium fill from below. Never reversed. |
| Palette | Desaturated blue-grey steel, rust brown, warm sodium orange |
| Rendering | Painterly semi-realistic, confident structure, restrained detail |
| Projection | Strict flat side elevation, orthographic, no vanishing point |
| Register | Closest to the **far** layer: low contrast, dissolving into haze |

Things this project has already tried and rejected, so they do not come back:
**no cel shading, no neon, no energy drawn as glowing geometry.** Energy in this
game is drawn as light, never modelled as an object.

Structural vocabulary that is correct for a yard, from the game's own canon:
girder-and-plate lattice, ore convoy docks and berths, foundry stacks, cargo
cranes, transit spars, catwalks, cable runs, and **lift spars** — the raked
structural masts underneath that hold the whole thing up. Stillwake had 140 of
them. They are the most characteristic silhouette a yard has and the reason it
should sit low in the circle with its underside showing.

## 4. Technical specification

These are the numbers the engine uses.

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Delivered file | `title_stillwake.png` |
| Delivered size | 2048x2048, square |
| Colour | RGBA with a real alpha channel |
| Outside the circle | Fully transparent (alpha 0) |
| Circle edge | Soft alpha falloff, **no hard rim** |
| Drawn size in game | ~520 px across, on a 960x540 logical screen |
| Drawn opacity | ~0.30, set in the engine — deliver at full opacity |

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

* **It is displayed at roughly a quarter of its delivered resolution.** Fine
  detail will not survive. Design at the scale that reads: large forms, clear
  silhouettes, broad tonal masses. The window lights should be points, not
  windows.
* **It is displayed at about 30 % opacity.** Anything subtle in the original
  becomes invisible. Anything high-contrast in the original becomes the only
  thing you can see. Aim the composition at how it looks faded, not at how it
  looks in the file.

### The keep-out zone

The lettering occupies a band across the middle. In the delivered 2048x2048
image, treat the **central horizontal third — roughly y 680 to y 1370, full
width — as a quiet zone.** Structure may pass through it, but only as large,
soft, low-contrast form. No bright lamps, no crisp edges, no fine linework, no
warm accents, and no hard silhouette boundary in that band.

## 5. What to do with what comes back

* **Check it faded, not at full strength.** Drop it to 30 % over a dark
  blue-violet background before judging it. That is the only view that matters.
* **Check the centre against the lettering**, not on its own. The failure mode
  is a medallion that is beautiful in isolation and eats the title.
* **Reject anything that reads as a disaster.** Tilt, fire, smoke plumes,
  breaking structure or falling debris are all wrong, and they leak a reveal.
* **Reject any version with a drawn ring or border.** It turns the piece into a
  badge sitting on the screen instead of a view opening behind it.
* **Alpha will likely need a hand pass.** Image models tend to produce either a
  hard circular cut or a muddy grey halo instead of clean transparency. A soft
  radial alpha mask in an editor fixes both, and is easier than re-prompting.
* Drop the finished file at `assets/ui/title_stillwake.png`, then set
  `TITLE_MEDALLION_ART` to `true` in `src/scenes/BootScene.ts`. **Both in the
  same commit.** Until that flag is flipped the engine draws its own placeholder,
  because a missing asset is served an HTML page by the dev server rather than a
  404, and the image decoder hangs on it forever — the game simply never boots.
