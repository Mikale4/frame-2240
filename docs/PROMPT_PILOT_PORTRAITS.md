# Pilot Portraits — Anime Restyle Brief

Paste this whole file to the image model, and attach the eight existing
portraits. It contains the prompt, the hard constraints, and everything about
each pilot that should show in their face.

---

## THE PROMPT

> I am attaching eight character portraits from an original 2D mecha combat
> game. I want each one **redrawn as traditional hand-painted anime key art, in
> the late-1980s/1990s "real robot" OVA idiom** — the grounded, adult,
> military-industrial end of the mecha genre, where the machines are equipment
> and the pilots are employees.
>
> Concretely, that means: **painted cel**, not flat cel and not airbrush.
> Confident heavy linework; two or three hard shading steps; but *painted*
> rendering inside those steps, with visible brush character in the hair and
> suit fabric. **Adult facial proportions** — real noses, defined jawlines,
> comparatively small eyes, visible age and wear. Hair drawn in defined clumps
> and strands rather than a soft mass. Muted, slightly desaturated industrial
> palette, with each character's accent colour doing the work.
>
> **Explicitly not:** modern light-novel or moe styling (large eyes, tiny noses,
> pastel skin) — this cast runs from early 20s to their fifties and includes a
> career miner and a twenty-year security veteran, and that vocabulary cannot
> draw them. Not heavy shonen stylisation. Not photoreal digital painting.
>
> **Keep each character's design exactly as delivered.** Same person, same age,
> same hair colour and cut, same suit, same colours, same accessories, same
> expression and bearing. I am changing the rendering style, not the character.
> Treat the attached image as the character sheet and the description below as
> the authority on intent.
>
> **Repaint it. Do not smooth or upscale it.** The most likely failure here is
> an output that is my original image de-pixelated — same pose, same crop, same
> flat lighting, just cleaner. That reads as a filter, not an illustration. The
> seven requirements below are what make it a painting:
>
> 1. **Hard shadow edges.** Two or three flat tone steps with a *visible
>    terminator* between lit and shadow — on the face, the neck, and every fold
>    of fabric. No airbrush, no soft falloff, no continuous gradients.
> 2. **Directional light with real cast shadows.** Key from the upper left, cool
>    blue rim from the upper right. The image must actually contain: a shadow
>    under the jaw falling onto the neck, a shadow from the hair falling onto the
>    forehead and brow, and shadows from any worn equipment falling onto the
>    chest and collar. **Cast shadows are most of what makes hand-painted art
>    look hand-painted.**
> 3. **Matte surfaces.** No glossy sheen on fabric, armour or equipment.
>    Highlights are hard-edged shapes, not shiny gradients.
> 4. **Varied line weight.** Heavy where forms overlap and along the shadow
>    side; thinning to nothing on lit edges. Never a uniform outline.
> 5. **Correct age.** Render the age given in the description. Subtle skin
>    texture, faint lines at the eye corners on the older pilots, real jaw
>    structure. Not a flawless idealised face.
> 6. **Hair in clumps.** A few large defined masses with one hard-edged highlight
>    band across them — not many thin individually-shaded strands with sheen.
> 7. **Worn fabric.** These are working garments in an industrial world. Subtle
>    weave, and light wear at collars and shoulder seams.
>
> **These are eight portraits in one set.** They must look like they were drawn
> by the same artist on the same afternoon: identical light direction (key light
> from the upper left, cool rim light from the upper right), identical line
> weight, identical eye rendering, identical level of finish. Eight good
> portraits in eight styles is worse than eight plainer ones that match.
>
> **Per image, deliver:** one bust — head, shoulders, upper chest — with the head
> occupying roughly the top two thirds of the frame. Square composition. Front or
> slight three-quarter view, and **use the same view for all eight**. Face and
> expression clearly visible; these are characters, not helmets. No weapons, no
> mecha, no background scenery.
>
> **Background:** flat, pure `#00FF00` green, edge to edge, with no shadow,
> gradient, glow or reflection falling on it. Do not anti-alias the character's
> outer silhouette against the green. It is a chroma key and it will be removed.
>
> **Resolution:** at least 1024×1024, square. I will downscale.
>
> **Original characters only.** Emulate the general style of the medium. Do not
> reproduce, quote, or approximate any existing anime character's design, face,
> costume or pose.

| | |

## The world, in four lines

The ground was lost generations ago; everyone lives on **yards** — industrial
platforms held above a permanent cloud deck by networks of lift spars. Five
powers share that sky. The year is **43 CE**. These eight are the pilots of the
eight combat frames currently in service. It is an industrial, worn, cold-
weather world: flight suits are working equipment, not costumes.

## The eight pilots

Accent colours below are each pilot's machine palette — portrait and mech are
meant to read as belonging together. **Keep the colours as delivered.**

### 1. NAIA VESK — `halcyon`
**Woman, early 30s. Concord Skyward** (the oldest authority; doctrine, uniforms,
certification). Balanced interceptor ace, and an instructor by choice.

Short dark hair. **Azure blue** flight suit with **hot orange** trim, collar
open, headset around the neck rather than worn.

*Bearing:* composed, professional, with a slight edge of arrogance. Third-
generation service — her grandmother signed acceptance work on the first frame,
her mother died in one. Eleven years without losing a hull, the longest unbroken
record in her service. She is exacting about procedure because a fault nobody
corrected in time killed her mother. **She should look like the best pilot in
the room and like she knows it, but tired around it.**

### 2. HALDEN ROOK — `nocturne`
**Man, late 40s. Emberline Pact** (a defensive alliance of yards; rebuilt
hand-me-down machines). Heavy assault veteran, oldest of the cast by six years.

Cropped grey hair. Heavy **deep crimson** armoured suit with **cyan** piping,
high armoured collar. **Burn scarring on his face and hands** — keep it visible
and keep it old, healed, unremarkable to him.

*Bearing:* unbothered, blunt, dryly amused. He held an evacuation lift open for
nine hours during the worst atrocity in this world's history and got 2,240
people out; he has never once mentioned the number in twelve years. He trains
new pilots now and has the lowest first-year loss rate in the sky. **Not
bitter, not haunted — a working man who did the obvious thing and is annoyed
you brought it up.**

### 3. KESTREL DOYNE — `vesper`
**Man, late 20s. Concord Skyward.** Marksman. Deliberately assigned outside any
squadron.

Long pale hair tied back. **Teal** flight suit with **sulphur yellow** optic
trim, and a **slim targeting monocle over one eye**.

*Bearing:* quiet, watchful, very still. Came to flying from six years of survey
work mapping structural lattice, which is why he reads terrain better than he
shoots — and he shoots well. Reports note he says nothing on the channel for the
opening minutes of most engagements. **Stillness is the whole character. He
should look like someone who has already chosen where he will be standing.**

### 4. BRUNN OKONKWO — `terramoth`
**Man, 50s. Ironwake Colliery** (an extraction concern that armed its mining
walkers). Siege platform pilot — industrial, not military.

Shaved head. **Ochre** work suit with **amber** hazard striping, heavy collar,
**welding goggles pushed up on the forehead**.

*Bearing:* broad, calm, immovable. Thirty-one years on the same class of
equipment, twenty-six of them cutting ore. He was offered a commission three
times and declined all three on the grounds that he already had a job. He
thinks frame combat is *less* dangerous than a seam collapse, because a collapse
gives you four seconds and combat gives you minutes. **He is at work. That is
the expression.**

### 5. MIRA HOLLOWAY — `cirrus`
**Woman, early 20s. Free Latitude** (not an organisation — independent crews,
salvaged frames, no ranks and no records). Skirmisher, youngest of the cast.

Messy light hair. Minimal **silver-white** suit with **cyan** running lights —
the lightest kit on the roster, barely armoured.

*Bearing:* quick, open, grinning. Yard-born. Built her first machine at
seventeen out of three wrecks and a stolen reactor. She redesigned her own
weapon and then published the improvement for free, with fitting measurements,
for anyone building the same frame. **Genuinely delighted to be here. The only
uncomplicated person in the cast, and the art should let her be.**

### 6. SAAR TENGKU — `obelisk`
**Man, 40s. Ironwake Colliery.** Bulwark — a shield pilot who holds a position
and does not leave it.

Braided dark hair. Heavy **violet** armoured suit with **pale lilac** glow at
the shoulders.

*Bearing:* steady, patient, defensive. Twenty years standing the same watch over
the same three yards, a posting he requested twice. Asked what he would do if
ordered to advance, he asked who would be holding the opening after he left.
Eleven commendation braids, each for a position held. **Patience, not
aggression. He is a door.**

### 7. RASA VOLKOV — `pyre`
**Woman, mid 20s. Ashfall Irregulars** (a contract force that works for
everyone and prices hull loss as a line item). Incendiary brawler.

Wild red-orange hair. Scavenged **industrial orange** gear, **mismatched
pauldrons**, **soot-marked face**, goggles with **amber** lenses pushed up or
worn.

*Bearing:* reckless, cheerful, a little unhinged. She holds her company's record
for contracts completed *and* its record for machines destroyed — four hulls
written off, four contracts fulfilled. Channel recordings have her still
chatting during final approach. She stopped requisitioning cleaning after the
second wreck and calls the soot part of the uniform; other crews have started
copying it. **Grinning, filthy, completely at ease.**

### 8. IO SENNA — `nulltide`
**Woman, 30s (assessed 36). Free Latitude.** Electronic warfare. There is no
personnel file on her anywhere — her record was deliberately erased by someone
who was never named.

Straight black hair with a blunt fringe. Near-black **slate** suit with
**electric cyan** circuit tracing, and a **thin sensor headset across one
temple**.

*Bearing:* detached, clinical, unreadable. Pilots who have fought her describe
the experience as *being catalogued*. She is the most dangerous person in the
cast and the least expressive. **Not cold as in cruel — cold as in a person who
has decided that being legible is a risk.** Give her the flattest, stillest face
of the eight; the restraint is the characterisation.

## Learned on the first pass — read this before generating

The first attempt (NAIA VESK) got the **character design exactly right** — every
accessory, the insignia, the beauty mark, the earring, the headphone cable
routing and the expression all carried faithfully. Design fidelity is not the
risk. **Rendering is.**

What came back was the original artwork *de-pixelated*: same pose, same crop,
smoothed. Every fault traced to that:

| Observed | What to ask for instead |
| --- | --- |
| Continuous airbrushed gradients on skin and fabric | Hard-edged tone steps with a visible terminator |
| No cast shadows anywhere in the image | Jaw onto neck, hair onto brow, equipment onto chest |
| Flat frontal lighting | Key upper-left, cool rim upper-right |
| Glossy plastic sheen on jacket and gear | Matte paint; hard-edged highlight shapes |
| One uniform thin outline | Thick-to-thin tapering ink |
| Idealised mid-20s face on an early-30s character | The stated age, with skin texture and wear |
| Hair as many thin shiny strands | Few large clumps, one hard highlight band |

**If you only enforce two things, enforce hard shadow edges and cast shadows.**
An image containing three genuine cast shadows stops reading as generated almost
immediately.

---

## What to check before accepting a set

1. Put all eight side by side. Does any one of them look like a different
   artist drew it? That is the failure mode that matters most.
2. Is the light coming from the same side in all eight?
3. Are the eyes rendered at the same level of detail in all eight?
4. Is the green background truly flat, with no glow or shadow cast onto it?
5. Is each character still recognisably the same person as the attached
   original?
6. **Are there real cast shadows** — under the jaw, from the hair, from worn
   equipment? If not, reject it; this is the fault that recurs.
7. Is there a hard edge between lit and shadow on the face, or does the tone
   slide smoothly from one to the other? It must be hard.
8. Does the pilot look the age they are written as, or ten years younger?
