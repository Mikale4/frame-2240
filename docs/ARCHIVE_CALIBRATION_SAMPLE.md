### `stillwake` — STILLWAKE
- **subtitle:** Concord Court of Inquiry summary, 32 CE · Archive addendum, 43 CE
- **tagline:** Finding: the programme met its trial objective.

**¶1.** The electronic-warfare programme was built to interrupt a yard's lift network. Frame combat was secondary to the requirement. A disabled frame retains some capacity to glide. A yard supporting 9,000 people maintains altitude through continuous reactor output distributed across 140 lift spars. The trial question was whether one airframe, operating outside the defensive envelope, could drive that network into cascade.

**¶2.** Fourteen programme airframes were completed. On the eleventh of the fourth, 31 CE, one was assigned to a live trial against Stillwake Yard. The yard was held by the Emberline Pact, remained inhabited and had been entered for review as a military target. Trial authorisation covered disruption of the lift network and observation of its recovery.

**¶3.** The cascade propagated in four minutes. Recovery became impossible nineteen minutes later. Stillwake settled through the remainder of the day, entered the storm layer and continued below it. Evacuation lift removed 2,240 people before access failed. The remaining population was lost with the yard.

**¶4.** The inquiry recorded Stillwake as a legitimate military target under review. It also recorded total lift loss outside the anticipated trial outcome. The two findings were filed separately. After Stillwake, neither side resumed the previous operational tempo. Formal closure of the Seven Yards War followed eighteen months later, in 32 CE.

**¶5.** The programme closed that year. Thirteen airframes returned to inventory and were struck. The fourteenth remained unaccounted for. The trial pilot was cleared, removed from the record instead of charged and omitted from subsequent programme disposition. The inquiry noted the removal, declined to reverse it and supplied no explanation.

**¶6.** Archive addendum: later field material identifies the missing airframe as EW-14 NULLTIDE in Free Latitude service. Concord recovery traffic contains no pursuit order. The addendum remains attached to the 32 CE programme closure file.

### `cirrus` — LT-21 CIRRUS
- **subtitle:** Free Latitude build copy · twenty-one recorded completions · revisions mixed
- **tagline:** Use the measurements on the frame in front of you.

**¶1.** There is no LT-21 production line. The pattern was passed from yard to yard and twenty-one completed machines appear in the copies collected here. Most builders worked separately. No two completion sheets match. The common instruction is to remove everything that is not thrust, then check what the thrust is still attached to.

**¶2.** Completed frames are the fastest in service and the most fragile. Boost recovery runs at twice the standard rate because little reactor output remains assigned elsewhere. Twin daggers carry an eight-hit sequence, longest of the current melee patterns. Earlier copies show four. Eight is the Holloway revision and was published without charge.

**¶3.** WISP flies independently and remains deployed beside the frame. It strings a charged line back to the LT-21. Put a target between frame and drone and carry the line through it. Free Latitude copies call this the scissor. A lost WISP may be rebuilt once. Several copies repeat that instruction in the margin.

**¶4.** Concord assessment classes the pattern as unsurvivable and declines certification. No Free Latitude submission for certification is attached. Local builders remain responsible for armour mass, thruster fit and the final weight entry on each completion sheet.

### `pyre` — RASA VOLKOV
- **subtitle:** Ashfall contract-performance ledger · four hull losses · current account
- **tagline:** Contract completion and asset loss, same ledger.

**¶1.** Volkov, Rasa. Contract pilot, woman, mid 20s. Four PYRE assignments are entered against her name. Four hulls were written off. No injury required extended stand-down. She holds the Irregulars' record for hull loss and their record for completed contracts. Accounts reviewed the pairing twice and closed both reviews after margin calculation.

**¶2.** Range acknowledgements are complete. The main gun remains effective to 300 metres and the rest of the loadout requires the same close approach. Channel recordings show conversation continuing during final entry. Debriefs record no request for revised tactics. Her stated position is that delay adds no useful reach.

**¶3.** Volkov burned two yards she had been contracted to defend. Both claims were ruled within terms. Neither client placed a second contract. Subsequent Ashfall booking volume remained unchanged.

**¶4.** Cleaning requisitions stop after the second written-off hull. Soot staining remains on her issued kit and she refers to it as part of the uniform. Similar marking now appears across Ashfall flight crews. The Irregulars continue to carry no uniform allowance.

### `nulltide` — IO SENNA
- **subtitle:** Archive reconstruction · Free Latitude dock records and third-party combat reports · revised 43 CE
- **tagline:** "Data point."

**¶1.** The Archive holds no Concord personnel file under Io Senna. Surviving programme material refers to a trials pilot around whom all fourteen EW airframes were built. She was certified at twenty-two and was the only pilot to fly one operationally. Cross-referenced ages and dates place Senna at twenty-four on the eleventh of the fourth, 31 CE, when she flew the Stillwake trial.

**¶2.** The court of inquiry described her record as removed. It did not identify the authority responsible. No charge was entered. The finding states that the trial was ordered, correctly briefed and correctly flown, and assigns the result to failure of the yard's lift network instead of pilot action. Programme references to the pilot cease when the airframes were struck in 32 CE.

**¶3.** Free Latitude dock records first place Senna and the fourteenth airframe together in 33 CE. Later entries identify the frame as EW-14 NULLTIDE. Remaining Concord traffic contains no recovery order, interception request or notice of pursuit. Free Latitude has continued to enter the machine as salvage.

**¶4.** Across eleven years of sightings, reports agree on three repeated behaviours: low-volume communications, disengagement from retreating opponents and cessation of fire once a target loses the ability to resist. Pilots who have fought her describe the encounter as being catalogued. The reports provide no further explanation.

**¶5.** Observed loadout remains consistent. Cryogenic and electrical payloads slow movement, drain systems and produce intermittent stalls. JAMMING FIELD halves the opposing frame's energy regeneration. STATIC remains deployed while projecting a separate false silhouette of NULLTIDE; drone, frame and projection may all be visible together. The projection causes no damage, though its imitation fire may stun. The arc glaive opens a five-hit cascade once the target has been slowed.

**¶6.** One personal remark survives in a Brightwake mechanic's statement. Senna asked why anyone would want to be remembered. The witness first entered the remark as evidence of detachment, later withdrew that interpretation and requested an amendment. The Archive records the remark and the withdrawal. Both remain attached to the same dock report.

Constraint note: The strongest pressure was `nulltide` pilot. Its combat mechanics had to remain explicit enough for player verification without turning the reconstruction into a hardware assessment or assigning Senna an interior state. `stillwake` also required a visible source-to-addendum seam; keeping that seam produced some deliberate repetition rather than concealing the canon chain.
