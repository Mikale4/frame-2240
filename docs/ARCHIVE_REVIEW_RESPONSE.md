## 3. Structural ruling: what the Archive *is*

You asked whether the Archive preserves one source document per entry, an
editorial compilation, or a mixture. The brief never answered this. It should
have.

**The Archive is a Concord-maintained records system that compiles material from
all five powers.** Mixture, under a stated convention:

- The **subtitle names the source document.**
- The **body** may be a source document, a compilation of several, or a source
  document with Archive addenda appended.
- **The Archive's editorial voice is Concord's, and is therefore not neutral.**
  This is a feature, not a leak.

This resolves most of the attribution conflicts at a stroke. The Archive can
narrate Ironwake's silence because the Archive is not Ironwake. It explains how a
Concord system holds an Ironwake works log. It is why Senna having no Concord
file is meaningful rather than merely convenient.

**Consequence for your point 8 and the brief's §4:** the self-referential voice
("the Archive is obliged to note") is **load-bearing**, not a tic. The brief told
you to cut it to ≤1. Revised instruction: **keep two or three**, placed where the
compilation seam is visible. Cutting it entirely removes the only signal that
these are assembled documents rather than an omniscient narrator.

**Subtitles — rewrite freely, and please do.** They should become filing
references: document type, originating office, date, revision status. This is the
cheapest evidentiary texture available and currently all 21 are literary.

**Taglines — rewrite aggressively, with two exceptions.**

> **PROTECTED — `stillwake`, "The programme worked. That was the finding."**
> You list this as an offender. It is the thesis of the entry. It may be reworded

Everything else is open, including the seven other pilot quotations. You are

quoted remarks with stated provenance.

Incidental: Vesk's current tagline ends "the one who is still here" and
Holloway's hurt banter is "Ow. Ow. Still here!". Unrelated lines — do not

| `nocturne` pilot | **Explicit about conduct, evasive about authorisation.** Rook's actions, the burns, the nine hours, the recovered frame, the testimony, the finding. Emberline holds this file and enjoys writing it. It should *not* explain what the trial was for: Rook deduced that from tapes, so the record states that he testified, not what Concord intended. |
| `nulltide` pilot | **Explicit about the erasure, opaque about the person.** The gap stated as a gap. Her age at the trial, the clearance, the departure with the airframe. No interior access. |

**Overlap is desirable, not merely acceptable — provided each retelling is
differently biased.** Four institutions describing one incident, agreeing on
facts and disagreeing on meaning, is the strongest evidentiary texture available
and it costs nothing. What must be avoided is four accounts that are
*complementary by design*, each supplying the next missing piece on cue. They
should overlap awkwardly, repeat one another, and differ on emphasis. The reader
assembles it. The record does not hand it over in order.
