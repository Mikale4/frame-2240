# Title Screen Key Visual — Art Brief

Paste this whole file to the image model. It contains the prompt, the
composition constraints the UI imposes, and the world and story context the
image should be true to.

---

## THE PROMPT

> Paint a **title-screen key visual for an original 2D aerial mecha combat
> game**, in the style of a **late-1980s/1990s "real robot" anime OVA poster**:
> hand-painted, cel-influenced, heavy atmosphere, muted industrial palette,
> dramatic scale. Machines are equipment, not superheroes. The mood is **awe and
> altitude** — cold void above, warm haze below.
>
> **The scene.** Night, high above an unbroken cloud deck. A vast abandoned
> industrial drydock hangs in the air: cathedral-scale steel girders and gantry
> columns receding into blue-grey haze, empty ship cradles, catwalks, hanging
> chains and cable runs. Scattered warm amber work lamps still burn along the
> structure — nobody decommissioned the reactor. Dust and moisture drift through
> the lamp light. Far below and behind, the tops of distant industrial platforms
> break the cloud layer as a thin silhouetted skyline. Above, cold clear void
> and stars.
>
> **The subject.** Two humanoid combat frames, roughly 8–10 metres tall, mid-air
> and mid-confrontation within the structure. They should read **small against
> the architecture** — the space must dwarf them. Semi-realistic hard-surface
> machines: boxy armoured torsos, exposed joint actuators, thruster housings on
> the back and hips, weapon hardpoints on the arms. Worn painted metal, panel
> lines, scorching, industrial wear. **Not glossy, not chrome, not organic, not
> super-robot.** One machine in **azure blue with hot orange trim**, carrying a
> glowing blue projected energy blade. One in **deep crimson with cyan piping**,
> heavier, carrying an autocannon.
>
> **Composition.** Wide 16:9 landscape. Leave the **upper third comparatively
> clear and low-contrast** — a game logo goes there and must stay legible. Leave
> a **clear horizontal band across the lower-middle** for a "press start" line.
> Put the visual weight in the middle band and the lower corners. Do not centre
> a bright element where the logo will sit.
>
> **Resolution:** 1920×1080 or larger, 16:9.
>
> **No text, no logo, no lettering, no watermark, no signature, no border.** I
> will add the title myself.
>
> **Fully original.** Do not reproduce, quote or approximate any existing anime,
> game, franchise, mecha design or poster composition.

---

## Composition constraints, restated

The game renders at **960×540 logical (16:9)**, so deliver 16:9 and it scales.

| Region | Reserved for | What the art needs to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top ~30 % | Game logo + subtitle + tagline | Keep it darker and low-contrast. No focal point. |
| Middle band | The machines, the structure | This is where the image lives |
| Lower-centre band | "PRESS START" and key hints | Keep it readable — avoid busy detail dead centre |
| Lower corners | — | Good place for foreground girders framing the shot |

A **dark vignette or gradient toward the top edge is welcome** — it is what
makes white title lettering sit cleanly over the image.

## The world

The ground was lost generations ago in an event called **the Descent** — the
official position is an industrial accident, and the other side records it as
*their* industrial accident. Everyone now lives on **yards**: enormous
industrial platforms held above a permanent storm-layer cloud deck by networks
of **lift spars**, each yard's altitude maintained by continuous reactor output.
Lose the lift network and the yard sinks into the storm layer and keeps going.

The year is **43 CE**. Five powers share the sky:

* **Concord Skyward** — the oldest authority. Published doctrine, central
  certification, committee-designed airframes. Blues and oranges.
* **The Emberline Pact** — nine yards in a defensive alliance, flying rebuilt
  machines transferred from Concord as war reparations. Deep reds and cyans.
* **Ironwake Colliery** — an extraction concern that armed its mining walkers.
  Ochres, violets, hazard striping.
* **Free Latitude** — not an organisation at all: independent crews flying
  salvage, no ranks, no central register. Concord's own paperwork calls them
  pirates in one document and authorised visitors in another, and both remain
  current.
* **The Ashfall Irregulars** — a contract force that works for all four others
  and prices hull loss as a line item. Scavenged industrial orange, soot.

The last declared war — **the Seven Yards War**, 27–32 CE — ended after a single
afternoon nobody will describe in full. A one-airframe electronic-warfare trial
was flown against an inhabited yard to see whether its lift network could be
driven into cascade. It could. The cascade propagated in four minutes; recovery
became impossible nineteen minutes later; the yard sank through the storm layer
with most of its nine thousand people still aboard. The court of inquiry
recorded the yard as a legitimate military target *and* recorded the total lift
loss as outside the anticipated outcome, and filed the two findings separately.

**That is the tone this image should carry**: industrial, cold, enormous, and
quietly wrong. Not heroic. Two machines fighting in a cathedral nobody owns.

## The place — CATHEDRAL YARD

The arena, and the setting for the key visual.

An **abandoned drydock**, built to take ore convoys and fit out airframes. Its
lift network was cut during the war's settlement and never restored, so the
lattice hangs on residual buoyancy and cannot support a population. Nobody holds
it, which is exactly why disputes get settled there — it is the one structure at
that altitude no power's claim covers.

The cradles still stand. The gantry lights still run.

**It is called a cathedral because of what the structure does to light and
scale**: enormous vertical girders receding into haze, the sense of a nave, a
space far larger than the thing fighting inside it. **The machines should look
small in it.** That is the single most important instruction in this document.

Established palette: cold blue-grey steel, deep night sky, warm amber lamps as
the only warmth, dust visible in the light shafts.

---

## The two machines to depict

Use these two — they are the game's poster pair and already flank the current
title screen.

**AV-07 HALCYON** — a balanced interceptor. Medium build, clean lines, the most
"standard" frame in the game. **Azure blue** armour with **hot orange** trim
and cyan detail lights. Carries a **projected energy blade** — a glowing blue
blade of light emitted from a hardware emitter on the forearm, not a metal
sword. Thruster housings at the back and hips.

**AV-13 NOCTURNE** — a heavier assault frame, an older airframe rebuilt from
salvage by a different faction, so it should look **visibly modified and
patched**: added armour plates, mismatched panels, field repairs. **Deep
crimson** with **cyan** piping. Carries a large **autocannon** and heavy claw
hardpoints. Slower, bulkier, more massive than HALCYON.

They are enemies but not villains — one is a Concord instructor, the other an
Emberline veteran who defected. Neither should be drawn as monstrous.

---

## What to check before accepting

1. **Do the machines look small?** If they dominate the frame, it has failed.
2. Is the upper third clear enough that white lettering would read over it?
3. Are the warm lamps the *only* warm light, against cold blue-grey steel?
4. Does it look painted and hand-made rather than 3D-rendered or airbrushed?
5. Is there any text, logo or signature in the image? There must not be.
