# Babel Sword: Reignition Archive Rewrite

## Editorial Diagnosis and Entry Plan

Prepared as the first two passes requested in `ARCHIVE_REWRITE_BRIEF.md`. No replacement lore prose is included here.

## Pass 1 — Diagnosis

The diagnosis in §4 is accurate, but it mostly measures sentence-level mannerisms. The deeper problem is that the Archive has one author pretending to be several institutions. The records-system conceit requires institutional limitation, prejudice and differing standards of relevance. Instead, every entry speaks with the same omniscient editorial intelligence. It understands procurement, battlefield performance, private motives, political hypocrisy and historical irony equally well.

### 1. The institutions are implausibly candid

Concord does not merely disclose its failures; it explains the precise moral contradiction behind them. Ashfall understands its own grotesque incentives. Emberline summarises its transformation from defensive pact to expansionist power with detached accuracy. Free Latitude supplies polished explanations for why its disorder works.

Real institutional prose lies differently. Concord would separate responsibility across findings, authorisations and technical failures. Emberline would call its expansion recovery or security. Ashfall would reduce culpability to contract terms. Ironwake would discuss equipment before politics. The brief identifies these intended voices, but the present entries do not actually inhabit them.

### 2. The metadata gives the game away before the body starts

All 21 taglines are written as miniature literary verdicts. The eight pilots each receive a polished quotation, as though every person in the setting naturally produces trailer dialogue. Even the institutional taglines arrive in the same dry, reversible form:

- “The programme worked. That was the finding.”
- “A HALCYON that was told it was wrong and agreed.”
- “It does not out-shoot you. It makes you unable to shoot.”
- “Data point.”

Changing only the body would leave the strongest evidence of common authorship untouched. Most taglines should become filing descriptions, clipped extracts, maintenance language or unremarkable statements. A few can remain memorable.

### 3. Every paragraph is a miniature argument

The architecture problem exists below the entry level. Individual paragraphs generally perform the same sequence:

1. State an apparent fact.
2. Qualify or reverse it.
3. Interpret what it “really” means.
4. Finish with a composed line.

That makes 89 small essays rather than 89 pieces of record. The paragraphs seldom contain a fact that the writer has not converted into a judgement.

Institutional prose often does less. It records a modification, gives a date, identifies an unresolved defect and moves on. Some paragraphs should have no thematic function beyond carrying information.

### 4. The text is too certain about private psychology

The files repeatedly convert interpretation into fact:

- Vesk’s fury and the exact nature of her arrogance
- Doyne’s stillness being something other than calm
- Holloway’s recklessness being fully reasoned
- Volkov’s cheerfulness being genuine
- Senna’s habits being divided cleanly into choices and reflexes

Those readings may remain canon facts, but the record should show how an institution knows them. A service evaluation can report conduct. A colleague can supply an observation. A board can make an inference. The current narrator simply enters people’s heads.

### 5. Every subject has been made uniquely exceptional

Across §8, “the only” appears repeatedly, alongside the fastest, oldest, longest, highest, most fragile and most dangerous. Some of those rankings are mechanically necessary, but the habit extends beyond the mechanics. Almost every machine and pilot receives a record, paradox or singular distinction.

The result resembles character-select copy: each entry proves why its subject is special. A records system should allow some subjects to be ordinary on paper. HALCYON in particular will improve if its entry genuinely treats adequacy as adequacy rather than turning adequacy into a superior philosophy.

### 6. The prose repeatedly advertises the game design

Terms such as “on the roster”, “two-hit chain”, “engagement envelope”, “passive” and rankings against every other machine make the institutional writers sound aware that they are describing selectable combat units.

The mechanic facts must remain, but their presentation can vary:

- Concord gives trial figures and certification results.
- Ironwake describes tolerances, duty cycles and modifications.
- Emberline writes field doctrine.
- Ashfall records cost, duration and expected loss.
- Free Latitude writes what fits and what breaks.

This is the main place where the canon lock applies pressure, but it does not require a canon change. The figures can survive as test or operating data rather than designer commentary.

### 7. The Stillwake material is sequenced like a revelation

The interlock itself is strong and must remain. The problem is presentation. STILLWAKE, NULLTIDE, Rook and Senna each release exactly the piece of information needed to deepen the same mystery. The disclosures arrive in escalating dramatic order and end with interpretive closure.

Records would overlap more awkwardly. The inquiry would use evasive terminology. Rook’s personnel file would state the consequence before supplying the event. Senna’s erased record would contain administrative discontinuities rather than a narrator explaining the shape of the erasure. NULLTIDE’s assessment would distinguish the airframe’s present combat rating from the yard-collapse programme without turning that distinction into an epigram.

### 8. There is almost no evidentiary texture

The prose mentions files, findings, ledgers and assessments, but rarely behaves like one. There are almost no document artefacts:

- inspection language
- test conditions
- copied remarks with uncertain provenance
- repeated administrative terminology
- clerical inconsistencies
- irrelevant serial or payroll details
- distinctions between confirmed, reported and assessed facts

The text tells us records exist while continuing to narrate smoothly. One quoted inspection line, one badly copied Free Latitude note and one visibly self-protective Concord finding will do more than adding general bureaucratic vocabulary everywhere.

### 9. Sentence openings and syntax are homogenised

In the current body text, 71 of 267 sentences begin with “The”. Another large group begins with “He”, “She” or “It”. This produces a repeated subject–verb–qualification rhythm even where the wording changes.

There are also very few parentheses, fragments, headings embedded in prose, formulaic references or ugly administrative sentences. Everyone has the same command of cadence. The Free Latitude records are grammatically cleaner than many professional reports.

### 10. The facts are suspiciously well curated

Even when a detail is mundane, it later explains character or doctrine:

- Vesk’s mother’s death explains her caution.
- Rook’s burns prove his conduct at Stillwake.
- Doyne’s surveying explains his combat method.
- Okonkwo’s seam experience explains his calm.
- Tengku’s watch duty explains his refusal to pursue.
- Senna’s programme history explains every present behaviour.

None of those links should be removed, but some should be allowed to sit without immediate interpretation. Readers can perform part of the assembly themselves. That differs from withholding information: the facts remain complete, but the institution stops announcing the meaning of every fact.

### 11. Date and figure handling lacks a governing convention

The text shifts among “31 CE”, “in 33 CE”, “the eleventh of the fourth, 31 CE” and fully spelled-out quantities. §4 catches the numbers as texture, but the larger issue is that no institution has a house style.

The rewrite should deliberately vary conventions while keeping every value fixed:

- Concord: `CE 31`, digits, elapsed times and formal findings
- Emberline: settlement and campaign dates, often tied to grievance
- Ironwake: shift dates, pattern numbers and service years
- Ashfall: durations, costs, loss counts and contract outcomes
- Free Latitude: inconsistent but comprehensible notation

## Constraint finding

No §3 constraint appears to require a canon amendment. The comparative mechanics are the likeliest source of artificial prose, but they can be expressed as institutional measurements without changing their meaning. Dates, figures, genders, ages, the Stillwake interlock and all eight mechanical profiles can remain intact.

## Recommended next-step gate

Before drafting all 21 entries, the original author should review the diagnosis and plan for:

- any canon implication the editor has misread
- any institutional attribution that conflicts with the intended source of an entry
- any tagline or quotation that must remain for narrative reasons
- any item in the Stillwake interlock that should be stated more or less directly
- whether to approve a full rewrite or first request a small calibration sample

A three-entry calibration set would be useful if the author wants to test the approach before approving the full pass:

- `stillwake`, for defensive Concord inquiry prose and canon-density
- `cirrus` machine, for deliberately rough Free Latitude writing
- `pyre` pilot, for flat Ashfall ledger prose without a terminal sting
