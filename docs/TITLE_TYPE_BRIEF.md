# Title Typography — Design Brief

**For an external designer or image model sketching wordmark directions for
*F.R.A.M.E. 2240*.**

This asks for **typography only** — six lettering designs, no background art, no
scenery, no illustration. The backdrop already exists and is not part of this
job.

This document is self-contained and assumes no access to the game.

---

## 0. The prompt to paste

**Self-contained — paste it on its own.** Attach this file only if the model asks
for more.

```
I need six wordmark designs for a video game title. TYPOGRAPHY ONLY - no
background art, no scenery, no illustration, no mascot, no logo mark. Just the
lettering.

The title is exactly:

F.R.A.M.E. 2240

Part 1 - What The Game Is

A 2D aerial mecha combat game. Single piloted machines fight each other in free
flight, one on one.

The world: the ground is gone. A permanent layer of storm covers a dead
surface, and everything that survives is built on enormous structures suspended
in the cold clear air two kilometres above it - lattices of girder and plate
carrying docks, foundries and whole populations, held up by reactor-driven
spars. Five powers hold territory up there. No war is currently declared;
disputes over docking rights and control of these structures are settled by
single combat between machines, under rules written case by case.

The machines are called frames. F.R.A.M.E. is an official designation the
world has worn down into an ordinary noun - nobody says the five words any
more, the way nobody expands "radar".

Tone: cold, industrial, grounded, procedural. Heavy engineering, not magic.
Steel, rivet, girder, sodium lamp, cold sky. There is a quiet sadness under it -
this is a diminished world doing paperwork about violence - but it is never
melodramatic and never heroic.

Part 2 - The Feeling To Aim At

Serious. Adult. Restrained. Machined rather than drawn.

NOT fantasy. NOT magical. NOT cyberpunk neon. NOT steampunk brass and cogs.
NOT military-shooter aggression. NOT retro-80s synthwave. NOT cute.

If the wordmark could sit on the side of a real industrial machine without
looking out of place, it is going the right way.

Part 3 - What I Need

SIX designs, all WILDLY different from each other. Do not give me six versions
of one idea with small variations. Each should be a genuinely separate answer
to the question, so that seeing them side by side presents a real choice.

Vary things like:
- Proportion. Very condensed and tall, or very wide and low. This changes a
  wordmark more than anything else.
- Weight and contrast. Massively heavy, or hairline thin, or heavy stems with
  hairline crossbars.
- Construction. Cut from plate. Stencilled. Engraved. Outlined only. Knocked
  out of a solid block. Assembled from segments. Welded. Stamped.
- How the two parts lock together. F.R.A.M.E. and 2240 do not have to sit on
  one line. One can be stacked over the other, set inside it, run vertically
  beside it, be enormous with the other tiny, or be separated by a rule.
- How the full stops in F.R.A.M.E. behave. They can be dots, squares, cut
  notches, tiny gaps, or almost disappear.
- Whether the lettering is contained - inside a plate, a bar, a border, a
  stamped frame - or floating free.

Part 4 - Hard Rules

- The text is exactly "F.R.A.M.E. 2240". Do not change the spelling, drop the
  full stops, or add or remove characters.
- DO NOT add a subtitle, tagline, translation, date, byline or any explanatory
  words. No extra text of any kind.
- DO NOT decorate with wings, skulls, crests, laurels, stars, gears, cogs,
  lightning, mecha, robots, or any illustrative element. Letters only.
- No background scenery. No sky, no clouds, no structures, no landscape.

Part 5 - Format

- One image containing all six designs, clearly separated, numbered 1 to 6.
- Plain flat black background.
- Render every design MONOCHROME - pale grey-white lettering on black. No
  colour, no gradients, no glow, no bloom, no metal reflections, no drop
  shadows. I am judging shape only, and colour hides weak forms.
- Give each design generous empty space. Do not crowd them.
- Each design should be legible at a glance and still readable if it were
  shrunk to a third of the size.

No text other than the title itself and the small numbers 1 to 6 labelling
each design.
```

---

## 1. Why monochrome, and why six

**Monochrome is not a stylistic choice, it is a test.** Colour, gradients and
bevels flatter weak letterforms; the current wordmark survives partly on a gold
ramp. Judged as flat shapes on black, a design either has structure or it does
not. Colour goes on afterwards, once the form is chosen.

**Six, wildly different**, because the failure mode of asking for options is
getting one idea six times. The prompt names the axes that actually change a
wordmark — proportion above all — rather than trusting "be creative" to produce
range.

## 2. What already exists

Not to be copied, and useful to know so the sketches do not accidentally
reproduce it:

* The current title is **drawn from original letterforms**, not a font: angular,
  chamfered, high-contrast, with a slight forward lean, filled with a warm gold
  ramp.
* `F.R.A.M.E.` sits high and left, `2240` low and right, on a shared diagonal.
* A slowly turning **gear train** sits behind the lettering, and a faded
  **silhouette of a floating city** sits behind that. Both are separate from the
  wordmark and stay whatever the lettering becomes.
* **The honest criticism of the current mark is that it reads as fantasy** — it
  would not look wrong on a role-playing game. That is the thing these six
  sketches exist to move away from.

## 3. The one thing that must not be revealed

**`2240` is not a year.** It is the number of people taken off a floating city
alive before it went down. The game withholds this until late, and a player is
*meant* to read the number as a date on the title screen.

So the wordmark must never gloss it. **No subtitle, no dates, no memorial
framing, no counter styling, no separating the digits, nothing that draws
attention to the number as a quantity.** It should read as a perfectly ordinary
title-screen number. That is why Part 4 forbids extra text outright rather than
explaining the reason — the reason is a spoiler.

Its expansion — *Field-Rated Aerial Manoeuvre Element* — is also deliberately
absent from the title screen, on the grounds that a world where nobody expands
the initialism should not have its title screen be the one place that does.

## 4. Technical constraints for whatever wins

Worth knowing, though the sketches do not have to honour them:

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Logical screen | 960x540 |
| Mark occupies | roughly the upper-left half |
| Background behind it | a dawn sky, mid-value, warm near the horizon |
| Must survive | being drawn at about 60 px cap height |

The mark sits on a **light-to-mid background**, not a dark one. A design that
depends on glow or on being lighter than its surroundings will not survive the
move from this brief's black sheet onto the real screen — which is exactly the
mistake that cost a round on the city artwork. Prefer designs that hold up as
*dark shapes* as well as light ones.

## 5. What to do with what comes back

* **Judge them small.** Shrink the sheet until each mark is thumbnail-sized. The
  ones still legible are the real candidates; that is the size a title reads at
  in a screenshot or a store listing.
* **Judge them inverted.** Flip the sheet to dark-on-light. A mark that only
  works in one polarity is fragile.
* Whatever is chosen gets **rebuilt as geometry**, not traced. The game draws its
  title from stroke skeletons in `src/ui/Logotype.ts`, so the winning sketch
  becomes a set of parameters and control points rather than an image file. That
  means the sketch needs to be *constructible* — clean, systematic letterforms
  beat a beautiful one-off painting here.
